2023. Six of us, one external client, one brief: work out why QTank's digital marketing
      wasn't converting as well as it should, and tell them what to do about it. This ran
      through ANU's Study Australia Industry Experience Program (SAIEP), supervised by
      Michelle Kramar, with QTank — a rural water tank and pump manufacturer — as the client.

It wasn't a software project. There was no repo to ship, no codebase to maintain. The
deliverable was a report and a set of recommendations, which made it a different kind
of pressure: nothing to debug, nothing to test against an oracle, just an argument that
had to survive being read by someone who runs an actual business.

**What we were asked to do:** audit QTank's recent marketing campaign performance and
turn it into a forward plan.

**What the data told us:**

- Engagement peaked on Sundays and carried through Monday and Tuesday before dropping
  off — not the weekday pattern a B2C assumption would predict.
- Impressions clustered in a 9am-12pm morning window, which became the recommended slot
  for paid placements.
- Two demographic clusters mattered most: homeowners aged 45-60 upgrading or maintaining
  property, and farmers managing small-to-large agricultural operations who needed
  durable, low-maintenance water storage.

**What we recommended:** a staged rollout rather than a single campaign — website
optimisation first, then targeted Facebook/Instagram campaigns aimed at the two
demographics, Google Ads PPC tuned to the morning engagement window, and a segmented
email sequence last, once there was an audience worth segmenting.

My part was mostly on the analysis side — pulling apart campaign performance by day,
time, and device, and translating that into something a non-technical stakeholder could
act on without a data dictionary. That translation step is harder than the analysis
itself. A regression or a pivot table doesn't help a client unless someone turns it into
"run this ad on Sunday morning, not Wednesday afternoon."

No code, no public repository for this one — the deliverable lives as a report and
presentation, not a thing you can clone.
