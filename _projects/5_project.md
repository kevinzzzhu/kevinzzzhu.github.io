This was my TechLauncher project, COMP3500, Semester 7, 2024, at ANU. TechLauncher is
ANU's industry-facing capstone unit: instead of a normal assignment with a fixed spec
and a marker, student teams are paired with an actual external client and have to
manage scope, communication, and delivery the way a small contracting team would. My
team's client was Yarn Speech, a Dart/Flutter speech app company — the same company I
later joined as a software developer intern, in a separate role.

Our team of around seven worked from a client briefing and a statement of work, then
ran our own sprint cycle: weekly stand-ups, a scribe rotation, a conflict-resolution
protocol we wrote ourselves, and milestones for design, build, and test phases over the
semester. We built and extended a Flutter app for Yarn Speech with audio recording and
a Supabase backend, along with a Python-side feature-extraction component for
processing recorded speech data. My own contribution sat across the Flutter front end
and helping coordinate the build-and-test milestones as we moved from a design-heavy
first third of the semester into implementation.

What stuck with me wasn't the code, it was the client relationship. A client has
opinions, changes priorities, and doesn't always know what they want until they see it
— very different from a spec written by a lecturer. Status updates and managing
expectations turned out to matter as much as the engineering itself, which is a lesson
a normal assignment doesn't really teach.

The client codebase isn't mine to share publicly, so there's no repo link here — but
the experience fed directly into the software-developer-intern role I went on to do at
Yarn Speech.
